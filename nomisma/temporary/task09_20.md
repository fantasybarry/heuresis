All-in cash-account simulation and exit comparison — September 20, 2026

Implemented the requested change for a $1,000 cash account. The new simulation requests all affordable whole shares of a qualifying stock, using settled cash and reserving the entry fee. The previous $100 account-drawdown requirement and financial sizing caps are removed. Drawdown is recorded without blocking entries or forcing liquidation. The existing momentum signal still determines eligibility; it is not a validated prediction that the stock will rise.

All ten registered runs are complete: nine policy/scenario comparisons and one exact repeat. Both protective exits reduced the historical loss substantially, but every scenario still lost money. Stable profitability has not been established. The exact repeat matched, independent reconciliation passed, and the final risk officer assessment found no material engineering defect. No exit policy was accepted for trading.

The new implementation is [nomisma/all_in_replay.py](../nomisma/all_in_replay.py), with [tools/all_in_replay.py](../tools/all_in_replay.py) as its command-line entry point. New regression coverage is in [tests/test_all_in_replay.py](../tests/test_all_in_replay.py) and [tests/test_all_in_replay_cli.py](../tests/test_all_in_replay_cli.py). [ALL_IN_EXITS_SPEC.md](../ALL_IN_EXITS_SPEC.md) contains the equations and event-order contract. The new configuration, registration and execution artifacts are under `artifacts/all-in-exits-20260920/`. Existing simulation files and historical task documents are preserved.

Requested quantity is `floor(available_settled_cash / (entry_cap + entry_fee_per_share))`. The former planning limit of 50 shares no longer determines quantity. Actual future quote capacity still limits fills; a partial entry cancels its remaining quantity. The model retains one filled entry episode per trading date, no later top-up, and the existing calendar-based settlement accounting. Reservations and unsettled sale proceeds cannot fund another entry.

All policies retain the initial 1.5-ATR stop, 2R target and 15-minute maximum holding time. ATR is frozen at the entry decision. Protective stops use valid bids observed after the fill, and execution occurs on later eligible quotes after modeled routing delay.

| Exit policy | Rule |
|---|---|
| `fixed_15m` | Original stop and target, with the 15-minute deadline. |
| `breakeven_1atr` | After a net marked gain of one ATR, raise the stop to entry price plus modeled entry/exit costs, rounded up. |
| `trail_1atr` | After the same activation, raise the stop to the greater of cost-aware breakeven and highest observed bid minus one ATR, rounded down. Never lower the stop. |

The fixed research inputs are ATR period 14, breakout window 5 bars, entry validity 120 seconds, minimum initial net reward/risk 1.5, one-cent price rounding and 25% executable quote capacity. The inherited planning `participation_rate` and legacy account risk/notional fields are explicitly ignored. Nominal per-share entry/exit fees and price displacements are $0.001; bar, quote, trade and routing delays are each one second. Cost stress doubles every cost, and timing stress separately doubles all four delays. These are documented simulation assumptions, not calibrated broker estimates.

The [registration](../artifacts/all-in-exits-20260920/registration.json) fixed three policies, three scenarios and one exact baseline repeat before the new results were seen. This is a diagnosis-informed comparison on previously exposed data, not an untouched validation study. No extra parameter search or outcome-driven rerun was performed.

The nominal entry requested and filled **199 shares** at $4.951. It reserved $995.199 and spent **$985.448, or 98.5448% of the account**, including its $0.199 entry fee. Residual settled cash was $14.552. Quantity was determined using the $5 entry cap before the eventual fill price was known; that explains why the realized cash deployment is slightly below 100%. All sale proceeds became available at the modeled September 15 settlement posting, and the final balances contain no reservations, unsettled proceeds or funding deficit.

The original exit sold 50 shares at $4.579, 25 at $4.579 and 124 at $4.559. Its larger loss is not simply the old 50-share loss multiplied by position size: finite quote capacity pushed the last 124 shares to a lower price. The observed $139.30 drawdown did not trigger an account-level control, as requested.

For the nominal protective exits, frozen ATR was $0.2346496914 and protection armed at approximately **09:02:13 Chicago / 10:02:13 Eastern**, when the observed bid reached $5.19. Breakeven raised the stop to $4.96. Trailing raised it through $4.96, $4.97, $4.98 and $4.99 as the observed bid reached $5.23. Trailing triggered about 0.000696 seconds earlier than breakeven, but both orders reached the same next executable quotes after their delay. Both sold 25, 50, 50, 25 and 49 shares at $4.949, $4.939, $4.929, $4.919 and $4.909, respectively. Both finished exiting around **09:03:46 Chicago**, versus **09:12:46** for the fixed stop.

This explains the negative result after a nominal breakeven stop: the stop is a trigger, and the delayed, capacity-limited execution prices were lower. Raising the stop by three cents did not improve realized proceeds in this opportunity. The fixed $5.68 profit target was never reached. A quoted peak gain is not an achieved exit, and the report does not assume a sale at the retrospective high.

Observed drawdown is a lower bound based on conditional quote marks. The fixed exit under doubled delays contained one unknown-equity interval lasting 0.748023096 seconds; the other comparisons had none. Zero reported unknown intervals does not establish continuously executable prices or complete market coverage.
