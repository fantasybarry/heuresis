RESEARCH WITH A REASON

A signal you can
look inside.

Nomisma screens historical daily price and volume data for bullish technical characteristics. Every result has an explainable score, the indicators behind it, and risks worth investigating.

The signal score is a weighted technical ranking from 0 to 100. It is not a probability that a stock will rise, and has not been calibrated as a return forecast. Historical strength can reverse.

01

Start with your universe

Choose the liquid-stock starter list, the provider’s active US exchange-listed universe, or your own symbols. Full-universe results can include ETFs. Uploaded CSV files use the symbols in the file.

02

Set the conditions for strength

Filter price, liquidity, relative volume, RSI, moving-average alignment, MACD momentum, 20-session returns, volatility, and sectors. Technical calculations require at least 201 ordered daily bars. Results that fail a selected condition are excluded.

03

Read the score, then the evidence

Scores combine transparent technical components. Open any stock to see its exact points and maximum weight for every component. Relative strength compares the stock’s 20-session return with SPY when benchmark data is available.

04

Keep the data in perspective

Live-provider scans use completed daily sessions, with a buffer after the market closes. IEX volume covers that exchange rather than the full market. CSV accuracy depends on the uploaded data. Demo prices are synthetic and use a fixed historical date.

Know your indicators

RSI · 14 sessions

Wilder’s relative strength index summarizes the balance of recent gains and losses. High values can signal strength or an extended move.

Relative volume

The latest session’s volume divided by the average of the prior 20 sessions. A value of 1.5× means 50% more activity than that baseline.

MACD · 12 / 26 / 9

The difference between the 12- and 26-session exponential averages, compared with its 9-session signal line.

ATR · 14 sessions

Average true range estimates recent price movement. ATR as a percentage of price helps compare volatility between stocks.

Moving averages

Simple averages of the latest 20, 50, and 200 session closes help describe short, medium, and longer trends.

Breakout distance

Measures where the latest close sits relative to the prior 20 sessions’ highest high. It describes a setup, not a confirmed future outcome.
