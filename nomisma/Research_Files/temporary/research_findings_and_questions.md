**Findings that matter most**

1. **The entry score describes momentum; it is not a calibrated forecast.** The earlier FTFT alert scored 85 after a 30.32% rise over five minutes and approximately 20.50% extension above VWAP. Several score components described that same recent move. The last prefill signal was still eligible, so a stale or disqualified signal was not established as that loss's cause. The useful question is whether the contemporaneous features predict subsequent net returns within the intended horizon. Source: [loss diagnosis, signal and geometry analysis](task09_17_loss_diagnosis.md).

2. **The broader results weaken the case for concentrating on exits alone.** The latest study contains 11 underlying entries on seven dates; its 66 primary trade records repeat those entries across six policies. All ten opportunities added by leniency lost under every tested exit. That does not prove the signal can never work, nor that every possible exit would lose. It does show that the six current exits did not rescue those entries. SES never reached its profit/protection levels; TE and GRAB hit their original stops under the five longer-limit policies. Source: [latest full comparison](task09_20_lenient_10000.md).

3. **Earlier profit-taking genuinely changed one recorded outcome, but did not establish an optimal rule.** On the original 199-share FTFT entry, the original exit lost $76.906, while the one-ATR target earned $51.194 and the two-minute exit earned $35.754. Those are valid modeled counterfactuals on one already-known path. A tighter initial stop still lost money, and a one-time positive-progress check allowed the later full loss. Selecting whichever historical peak or rule looks best is not independent validation. Source: [early-exit investigation](task09_20_early_exits.md).

4. **Entry reward/risk and actual early-exit payoff are different.** The admission filter uses the original stop/2R geometry. Smaller profit targets and timed/protective exits change the payoff distribution. The early-exit report's simplified ideal executions imply reward/risk of approximately 0.346 and 0.648 for the two small targets, requiring approximately 74.3% and 60.7% winners in a two-outcome model. Those figures are not measured win rates; delayed fills and variable losses change them. A high original admission ratio cannot substitute for measured after-cost expectancy under the actual exit policy. Source: [payoff caveat](task09_20_early_exits.md).

5. **Capital and position size amplify exposure; they do not create an edge.** Strict FTFT's two-minute comparison changed from 199 shares and +$35.754 at $1,000 to 950 shares and +$185.950 at $10,000. The larger account requested 1,999 shares but filled only 950, using $4,704.40. Its account return fell from 3.5754% to 1.8595%. Neither shares nor profits scaled tenfold. The old 50-share restriction was a planning cap in an earlier model, not a finding that the market could never support a larger trade. Sources: [cash-account history](task09_17.md), [all-in implementation](task09_20.md), [capital controls](task09_20_lenient_10000.md).

6. **A profitable trigger and a profitable fill are different outcomes.** The original FTFT $5.08 target produced a $4.999 fill after modeled delays. In the newer SERV example, reaching a $4.42 target/protection level still led to sales at $4.409 and a $2.50 loss on that episode. A 900-second exit deadline produced completion as late as 914.576798083 seconds. These mechanisms can materially alter short-lived gains. Resting limit exits would require a separately specified non-fill/queue model; touching a price cannot be counted as a sale. Sources: [early exits](task09_20_early_exits.md), [latest execution examples](task09_20_lenient_10000.md).

7. **The practical one-account stock selector remains unresolved.** Early cash-account runs chose the first feasible alert with deterministic ties. Later comparisons deliberately gave each stock/rule its own alternative account. They do not establish which symbol one shared account should select when several are eligible, whether it should wait, or how it should compare candidates using only information then available. A universe-wide strategy must test that decision with shared cash and settlement, without selecting the eventual winner retrospectively. Sources: [selection diagnosis](task09_17_loss_diagnosis.md), [six-stock account scope](task09_20_six_rules_six_stocks.md).

8. **The largest evidence gaps are still open.** The main replay dates remain the same nine exposed September dates. The latest cohort retains retrospective membership, a coverage-selection amendment and 934 missing regular-session minute slots. The older 20-session acquisition was minute-only and did not create a full execution-ready holdout. A three-minute prospective recording demonstrated a recorder, but its clock accuracy was unmeasured and 382 of 718 quote/trade timestamp differences were negative. One-second delays, zero fill-report delay, displayed-capacity fractions and settlement posting assumptions are not broker-calibrated facts. Sources: [data/recorder history](task09_17.md), [execution proposal](task09_19.md), [cohort limitations](task09_20_six_rules_six_stocks.md).

9. **Engineering validation is strong within the tested assumptions.** Deterministic repeats, ledger reconciliation, settlement, feasibility-before-reservation, future quote-backed fills and explicit unknown valuations substantially improved credibility. The latest study passed 98 targeted tests and 152 audit groups. Older full-suite counts such as 525 or 544 and newer focused counts are version/scope-specific, not a performance score or independent statistical sample. Historical approvals of offline reports and rejections of trading acceptance concern different scopes; they are not contradictory endorsements of profitability.

**What is current and what is historical**

The current requested research setup is $10,000 initial simulated cash per alternative account, a cash account, qualifying buy signals, all-affordable share requests subject to actual modeled fill capacity, and intraday exits around 15 minutes. The $100 account-drawdown control remains removed. These settings do not change the actual broker account's funding. The prior $1,000 studies, old risk caps, 30-minute development leader and six-hour exploration should remain preserved as historical evidence rather than being read as current instructions.

The P&L figures across files are not a cumulative equity curve. They differ in capital, dates, settlement rules, sizing, entry admission, policy scope and exit behavior. Similarly, final loss, worst pre-request mark, observed peak-to-trough drawdown and return on deployed cash measure different things. The earlier $5.23 quoted high concerned a shorter holding window, while the later near-close sale around $8.18 concerned a different window; that is not a price-history contradiction.

**Proposed priorities to discuss**

| Priority | Research objective | Concrete output before further tuning |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Define what the strategy must predict and how improvement will be judged. | One primary after-cost success measure, activity expectations, and a fixed evaluation protocol. |
| 2 | Make the simulated execution contract match the intended broker/workflow. | Explicit order types, price precision, fee assumptions, latency evidence, capacity/non-fill treatment and acknowledged unknowns. |
| 3 | Establish fresh evidence with a universe chosen before outcomes. | Coverage-qualified stock/date data, exclusions retained, and a later untouched evaluation set. |
| 4 | Diagnose entry discrimination and selection. | An alert-to-fill dataset covering accepted and rejected opportunities, decision-time extension/volatility/spread/volume, and predefined tests of subsequent net outcomes. Never invent fills for rejected opportunities. |
| 5 | Attribute changes and evaluate one shared account. | Separate cap-only and reward/risk-only changes; compare a small fixed entry/selection family with shared settled cash, then evaluate compatible exits. Keep the full trial record and freeze a candidate before final evaluation. |

Some groundwork can proceed together: specify the execution contract and register the data/evaluation scope while preparing entry diagnostics. No new parameter value or strategy is selected by this review. The highest-value improvement may be learning which setups to reject or when to remain flat, rather than increasing the number of simulated trades.

**Questions for discussion**

1. **What should be our primary definition of success: higher average net profit, more consistent results across fresh weeks, or smaller severe losses?** We should choose the main measure before comparing the next results. Your previously removed $100 cap is not being reinstated.
2. **How much of the entry logic should be open to redesign?** Should we retain the current breakout score, or investigate features that distinguish fresh continuation from an already extended move? The reports support examining this question but do not yet identify a profitable replacement.
3. **How should one account choose among eligible stocks?** First feasible signal, highest contemporaneous score, or a ranking that includes spread/liquidity and a validated estimate of net opportunity? The latest separate-account results do not decide this.
4. **Is the approximately $0.50–$5 stock universe a firm requirement?** Or may the next prespecified universe include stocks above $5 that meet stronger liquidity and data-coverage criteria? Expanding the universe would be a new hypothesis, not a presumed improvement.
5. **Which broker and order workflow should this system actually represent?** Broker, entry order type, exit order type and whether triggers are handled locally or by the broker determine which execution assumptions we need to verify.
6. **What market-data access and acquisition budget are available for genuinely new evaluation data?** That determines the feasible breadth of fresh stock/date observations and an untouched final test, rather than another sweep over the same nine dates.

The most consequential discussion is the combination of success criteria, entry definition and one-account stock selection. Broker/workflow and fresh-data scope determine whether the resulting test is credible.
